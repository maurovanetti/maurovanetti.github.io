[Jensen Huang's reinstatement at the helm of Nvidia](https://marxist.com/the-return-of-jensen-huang) was essentially decided by the New York Stock Exchange and Donald Trump. The former signalled, through a plunge in Nvidia shares, that getting rid of Huang — and thereby seriously hindering the export of the best microchips to China — was not welcome and risked bringing the whole house of cards down. The latter effectively ordered Nvidia's board to put Huang back in his place, along with his harem, assiduously devoted, so to speak, to facilitating business relations with China.

This development indicates that the collective decision-making process of the ruling class of the US superpower, across its economic, political, and military branches, has chosen the victory of the faction seeking to prop up [the AI financial bubble]({% post_url 2025-12-04-tre-bolle %}) a little longer, prioritising the interests of who makes its hardware over the interests of who makes its software, even at the risk that hardware sales could allow China to catch up. For now, the losing faction is the apocalyptic one, which predicts that Artificial General Intelligence will become an evil deity and kill us all — a prophecy in camouflage that in reality expresses, more plainly, the fear that China will outstrip the US, and therefore calls for measures to entrench today's monopolists in a privileged position on the pretext that they (Anthropic, OpenAI, and the whole gang of big tech multinationals) develop AI responsibly.

If this line were to prevail permanently, it would amount to meekly accepting Chinese competition in this sector too, while trying to make as much money as possible before everything blows up (in Trump's case, the profit is direct, since he has invested a lot of money in Nvidia). That outcome is far from certain: the Democratic Party is more belligerently anti-China and thinks more strategically, so it is unlikely to welcome giving Beijing the tools to strengthen its military with AI. On this point, it has allies among Republicans too, including some in the administration. Moreover, Nvidia cannot keep racking up billions on the stock market at this pace: its financial plan is unrealistic, resting on projections of building vast numbers of data centres — a physical impossibility — and fairy-tale profits for which there isn't enough money on the planet. When Wall Street, the very place where its ambitions were inflated out of all proportion, scales them back, Nvidia's political clout will shrink as well.

A constant feature of the current situation is that whatever the main actors do, they sharpen the contradictions that threaten the ground on which they stand. Letting China through is a temporary boost for Nvidia shares, since it means full access to an enormous market, but at the same time it weakens the dominance of American LLMs, which underpins the financial and technological boom that has lifted Nvidia itself. Conversely, making war on China — commercial, spy-based, or worse — might have prompted the so-called Communist Party in China to change its mind about exporting gallium, germanium, and antimony and reinstate its embargo on the US; without rare earths, chips cannot be made. Instead, perhaps also as thanks for putting Huang and his talkative mistresses back in place, Beijing has extended the suspension of the embargo, and trade can continue. After all, navigating this kind of storm is the essence of capitalism's survival techniques. But there is always a vital, creative force that may prove impossible to tame: us, the working majority of the human species. If AI is so important, why don't we take it back?
